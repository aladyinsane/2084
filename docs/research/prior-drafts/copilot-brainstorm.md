# 2084 – Worldbuilding & Feature Inspiration
A design brainstorm for a text‑based dystopian MUD inspired by Orwell’s 1984, built in Evennia with CircleMUD‑style mechanics.

## 🎮 Core Design Philosophy
2084 shouldn’t be “1984 with swords.” The world itself should be the primary antagonist. Instead of XP grinding or PVP dominance, the game should reward subversion, survival, information control, and psychological manipulation. The player’s greatest weapon is knowledge — and the greatest threat is being known.

## 🧠 Unique Gameplay Systems
### 1. Surveillance‑Driven Progression
Instead of XP, players gain “Influence” or “Access Level” by interacting with the surveillance state.

#### Examples:

Hack or reroute telescreens.

Plant false data in citizen files.

Manipulate facial‑recognition archives.

Earn temporary “blind spots” where the regime cannot track you.

This creates a gameplay loop where information is the currency.

### 2. Dynamic Propaganda Engine
The game generates propaganda based on player actions.

If players sabotage infrastructure:

News reports blame “foreign saboteurs.”

NPCs become more paranoid.

Patrols increase.

If players spread anti‑regime ideas:

Posters appear warning about “thought‑infection.”

NPCs begin reporting each other.

This makes the world feel alive and reactive.

### 3. Memory Integrity System
Inspired by the Ministry of Truth.

Players have:

True Memories (what actually happened)

Official Memories (what the regime says happened)

Certain actions or encounters may:

Rewrite your memories.

Erase skills tied to forgotten events.

Create false memories that unlock new abilities.

This allows for identity‑based progression.

### 4. NPC Suspicion & Paranoia
NPCs track:

Your behavior

Your speech patterns

Your affiliations

Your presence in restricted zones

Suspicion levels affect:

Access to services

Willingness to talk

Whether they report you

Whether they betray you

NPCs can become:

Informants

Allies

Double agents

Broken shells of fear

### 5. Secret Language & Coded Communication
Players can invent or learn:

Ciphers

Slang

Symbolic graffiti

Hidden meanings in mundane phrases

This allows:

Covert communication

Hidden channels

Secret societies

The regime may eventually decode these languages, forcing evolution.

### 6. Social Trust Networks
Instead of guilds, players form cells.

Cells have:

Trust scores

Risk profiles

Shared secrets

Collective missions

If trust collapses:

Cells fracture

Members defect

Secrets leak

The regime cracks down

This creates emergent drama without traditional PVP.

### 7. Identity Erosion & Reconstruction
Your identity is not fixed.

Factors:

Regime pressure

Psychological trauma

Memory rewriting

Social manipulation

Identity affects:

Skills

Access

NPC reactions

Faction alignment

Players may:

Lose their name

Gain a new one

Become “unpersons”

Rebuild themselves through rebellion

### 8. Bureaucracy as a Game Mechanic
The regime is a machine of paperwork.

Players interact with:

Permits

Forms

Authorizations

Denials

Misfiled documents

Lost records

You can:

Forge documents

Misroute requests

Create bureaucratic deadlocks

Weaponize inefficiency

This is a uniquely dystopian mechanic rarely seen in MUDs.

### 9. Psychological Pressure & Stress
A subtle system that tracks:

Fear

Hope

Paranoia

Resolve

High fear:

Reduces combat ability

Increases mistakes

Makes NPCs distrust you

High resolve:

Unlocks rebellion actions

Improves stealth

Strengthens memory integrity

This adds emotional depth to gameplay.

## 🛠 Development Notes (Evennia + CircleMUD Style)
### Evennia Strengths
Python-based (easy to extend)

Flexible database models

Custom command parsing

Web client integration

Good for narrative-heavy systems

### CircleMUD Mechanics to Borrow
Room-based movement

Simple combat loops

Skill/spell cooldowns

Equipment slots

Prompt customization

### Where You Should Diverge
Replace XP with Influence/Access

Replace levels with identity tiers

Replace alignment with loyalty/suspicion

Replace guilds with cells/networks
